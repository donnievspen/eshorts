Three Chapters

Chapter 1 — An Ordinary Morning

Julie was barely out of the driveway when Mina heard footsteps on the stairs.

That was unusual. On weekdays, he was normally gone before the boys finished breakfast. If he was home, he was on the phone, looking for his keys, or asking a question while already walking away from the answer.

This morning he stopped at the bottom step in an old gray T-shirt and looked around the living room.

“Did you lose something?” Mina asked.

“I'm trying to figure that out.”

“Your briefcase is by the door.”

“Don't need it today.”

“Your meeting with the editor?”

“Ten. Online. I sent the pages last night.”

“Then I think you're ready.”

He looked at her, then at the clock. “It's eight-nineteen.”

“Congratulations. You have forty-one minutes to do nothing.”

“I'm not sure I know how.”

Mina smiled. She'd worked for the family long enough to know that was probably true. Julie had left a note about the children's bags, the laundry, and a grocery delivery, but none of it needed him. For once, the house could run without his supervision.

“I can take the boys outside,” she offered. “You could get ready without somebody asking you for something.”

“Are you suggesting I hide from my own children?”

“Only for a little while.”

He laughed, rubbing the back of his neck. He looked tired, she realized. Not the usual impatient kind of tired. His hair was flattened on one side and he hadn't shaved. It was strange seeing him without the polished version of himself he presented to the world.

“Coffee's fresh,” she said. “I'll put a mug on your desk.”

“Thank you, Mina. I don't know what we'd do without you.”

“Probably spend a lot more time looking for shoes.”

He smiled and headed back upstairs.

Mina opened the sliding door and called the boys into the yard. In the dark reflection of the glass she caught him stopping near the kitchen, his face turned toward her. She couldn't tell whether he'd been watching her or simply thinking about something. She went outside before she could decide.

Otto had discovered Mrs. Alvarez's cat in the oak tree. Owen wanted to know whether the cat was stuck. Mina assured them it was not, although she wasn't especially confident about that.

For the next twenty minutes she helped the boys build a lopsided fort from patio cushions. She was brushing dirt from her shorts when she heard her name through the open slider.

“Mina?”

She stepped into the kitchen. “Yes?”

“Up here. Sorry.” His voice came from the upstairs bathroom. “I forgot a towel. Could you grab one from the closet?”

“Of course.”

She took a clean towel from the linen closet and stopped outside the bathroom. The shower was running.

“Where should I leave it?”

“Just inside the door is fine. Sorry about this.”

She opened the door enough to slip in. Steam covered the mirror. Behind the frosted shower screen she could make out the blurred shape of his body and one arm raised against the wall. She tried to keep her attention on the sink, but kept staring at his outline. She caught her hay falling and quickly snapped out of it, placing the towel on the counter.

“It's right here.”

“Thanks. You're a lifesaver.”

“Apparently that's my job description.”

He laughed. She shut the door.

In the hallway she stood still for a second, irritated by how flustered she felt. She had delivered a towel. That was all. She'd seen less than she would have seen at a crowded swimming pool, and even that had been through frosted glass.

She went back outside. The boys had abandoned the fort and were now arguing about who owned a stick.

Later, while they were washing up, he came downstairs in gray joggers and a clean shirt. He asked about the children, looked out at the yard, and made some comment about the weather. Mina answered, but she noticed that his attention wandered back to her more than once.

He went upstairs again. A few minutes later he returned, carrying a mug he apparently had no intention of drinking from.

“Change of plans,” he said. “My afternoon's free. How would you all feel about going to the beach?”

Mina blinked. “Today?”

“Unless you have a better day in mind.”

The boys heard the word beach from the other room and came running. That settled it.

While he fetched the cooler, Mina collected towels, sunscreen, water bottles, and the spare clothes the children would inevitably need. She was halfway through checking the bag when he appeared beside the kitchen counter.

“Do you have a swimsuit?” he asked. “Julie has extras if you need one.”

“I brought one.”

She hadn't meant to answer quite so quickly. His mouth twitched as if he noticed.

“Great. Then we're set.”

Mina zipped the bag and tried to concentrate on the children's shoes. It was only a trip to the beach. They'd gone plenty of times with Julie.

But Julie wasn't coming today.
