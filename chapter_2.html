Three Chapters

Chapter 2 — The Beach

By the time they reached the beach, Owen had asked whether they were almost there fourteen times. Mina had counted because it was easier than thinking about the man driving.

The morning was bright and breezy. They found a place past the crowded umbrellas, where the sand was firm enough for the cooler and the boys could build their castle without trampling anyone else's towels.

“Sunscreen first,” Mina told them.

Otto groaned. Owen tried to negotiate. Neither succeeded.

Once the boys were safely occupied near the water, Mina sat beside the cooler and took a long drink. He unfolded a chair next to the blanket, removed his shirt, and asked whether she thought the tide was coming in.

She answered correctly. She was fairly sure of that. She was less sure she'd looked at the water while answering.

It bothered her how much she was noticing him. She'd known him for years. She'd seen him carry groceries, fix a cabinet, lose arguments with a seven-year-old, and fall asleep in front of the television. None of those memories had prepared her for the sudden difficulty of sitting beside him in the sunshine.

She'd worn her swimsuit under her clothes, but now she had to take those clothes off. She stood, unbuttoned her yellow shirt, folded it over the bag, and stepped out of her shorts. The pale blue suit had seemed perfectly ordinary when she packed it. She found herself wishing she hadn't spent so long choosing it.

“Nice day for this,” she said, sitting down.

“Definitely.”

She couldn't tell whether he was looking at her. She could have checked. She didn't.

When she leaned forward to retrieve her water bottle, her knee brushed his. Neither said anything. He shifted in his chair, then picked up the sunscreen.

“Would you mind getting my shoulders? I always miss the middle.”

“Sure.”

He turned away and she squeezed lotion into her palm. It was cold from the cooler. She spread it across his shoulders, concentrating on doing the ordinary job he'd asked her to do. His skin was warm, and the muscles moved slightly as he reached for his sunglasses.

“Too much?” she asked.

“Not at all.”

She finished the other shoulder and handed back the bottle.

“Thank you,” he said. “I'm going to test the water.”

He jogged down to the shoreline and waded in. Mina watched him dive beneath a wave. When he surfaced, he slicked his hair back and turned toward the beach.

She should have looked away then. Instead she watched him walk back.

His gray swim trunks had been loose fitting when he left the blanket. Wet, the fabric clung to his body. She could a perfect outline of him and she couldnt stop staring. She looked up just as he reached for his towel.

“Water's perfect,” he said, drying his face.

“Good.”

“You should go in.”

“I will.”

She unscrewed her bottle, took a drink, and nearly spilled it down the front of herself. He seemed to be hiding a smile behind the towel.

“I'm going to check on the boys,” she said.

“Good idea.”

She walked down to the water, where Otto was attempting to build a moat that the tide destroyed every thirty seconds. Mina helped him for a while. It was easier to discuss the engineering failures of a sandcastle than the way she'd just behaved.

When she returned, he was sitting upright in the chair, reading a message on his phone. He looked up.

“You're getting a little pink,” he said, pointing toward her shoulder.

Mina touched the skin. He was right. She reached for the sunscreen and worked it over her arms and shoulders, then tried to reach the middle of her back. Her fingers stopped short.

He watched her struggle for a moment.

“Want some help?”

She held out the bottle. “Please.”

He sat forward and she stood with her back to him. She could hear the cap click open. Then he paused.

“Ready?”

“Yes.”

His hand settled between her shoulder blades. She was expecting cold lotion; the warmth of his palm startled her enough that she shifted her footing in the sand.

“Sorry,” she said.

“Got you."

He caught her before she fell forward. His hand landing just below her breasts. His finger tips slipped up her bathing suit slightly and her pressed into the soft skin of her underboob. His hand lingered for a moment before he withdrew it.

“All done.”

She took the bottle over her shoulder. “Thanks.”

“You're welcome.”

She didn't turn around immediately. The boys were laughing at the waterline, and someone farther down the beach was calling for a missing sandal. Everything around them continued as normal.

“Julie will be home this afternoon,” he said.

“I know.”

“We should probably start packing soon.”

“Right.”

She walked toward the boys and called them in. She had spent the morning wondering whether she'd imagined the looks, the pauses, and the awkward silences.

Now she was fairly sure she hadn't.

She wasn't at all sure what to do about it.
